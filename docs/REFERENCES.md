## Notes: Kallaway split style (DZU1D_8OI62, 62.5 s), studied 2026-11-14
1. **Layout:** a light grid-paper panel (cool off-white, fine gray grid) on the top ~60%; the creator in a rounded card
   on the bottom ~40%, filmed in a dim room with practical lights (contrast against the white panel).
2. **Section title** at the top: 2 lines, line 1 black bold sans with one **serif-italic accent word underlined in red**
   ("Claude Just *Changed*"), line 2 bold red ("Social Media Forever"). It changes per section ("Paste URL And Get /
   **Every Secret** In That Video").
3. **Panel content:** real product UI (the Claude logo, a prompt box with `/analyze`, a pasted URL, the output document,
   a phone mockup of a reel), large and centred. Annotated screenshots with **hand-drawn words + squiggle arrows**
   ("Trends", "Insights", "Patterns").
4. **Captions:** 1 to 2 words at a time ("how you", "into", "Most", "/analyze", "video"), white bold sans, centred on
   the seam between the panel and the creator card. No big caption blocks.
5. **Rhythm:** about half the beats cut to the creator **full screen** with a single-word caption at mid-lower frame;
   the rest are split. That's a layout change every 2 to 4 s.
6. **The Double Tap** (DfHxna7R17Q caption): say each key idea twice, precise then simple ("The algorithm uses audience
   matching to feed topics to interested viewers. Think of it like a matchmaker…").

## Notes: captions-mixed-type (DYOPqxMvRRl, 52.7 s, 1080x1920), studied 2026-11-14

It's a caption-style showcase. What to borrow:

1. **Mixed-type stacks.** Small sans words sit on top of and under one huge serif word:
   "Your / **captions** / shouldn't", then "just be / **readable**". The keyword is 3–4x the size of the filler words.
   The stack builds word by word as it's spoken. Earlier words stay up and new words tuck in around the big one.
2. **Keyword in colour.** "EMPHASIZE **KEYWORDS**": bold condensed caps in white, with the keyword in bright green.
3. **Script accent.** "Have you / *seen* / people": the middle word is an italic script in green, overlapping the
   lines above and below. The next word ghosts in (gray, semi-transparent) before it's spoken.
4. **Two-word stacked pairs.** "no / **doubt**": a tiny word over a big bold serif word. "WAYMORE" is wide caps, one line.
5. **Placement.** Centred horizontally, from about mid-frame to two-thirds of the way down. Never under the UI zone.
   One phrase is on screen at a time (2–5 words), then a hard cut to the next.
6. **Motion.** Words pop in on the spoken word with a quick scale-up (no bounce trail). Keywords land slightly
   bigger, then settle.

## How this maps onto the paper-motion style

- Paper style already has the right bones: `.l1` sans setup words plus `.l2` big bold-italic serif punch words that ghost in.
- New for the Discord reel: a **big caption stack** at mid-frame for keyword beats. One huge serif or condensed keyword
  (ink, or the rust `#b4532f` / green `#3f8a55` palette instead of the reference's neon green), with small sans
  filler words stacked around it, popping in on the spoken word.

## Notes: hil-story-style (@hilzuckerman, DfWHPChtG_d, 143 s, 1080x1920), studied 2026-11-15
"How to make boring content addictive". 10.9K likes, 324 comments, 286 reposts, **6.3K sends** (a send-heavy reel).
Studied frame by frame (1 frame/s contact sheets in the browser; not downloaded). Kept: the storytelling arc and the
caption fonts (`pop-serif`). The full-screen layout became a `story` style that is kept as style-03.

**Look**
1. **Face cam full screen the whole time** (sitting on a bed, warm lamp glow, handheld mic). No split panel.
2. **Captions:** one word at a time at chest height, Poppins Bold white with a soft shadow, no box. The important word
   switches to a **serif italic in pale butter yellow** ("*interesting.*", "*changes*", "*hundred*", "*interest*").
3. **Top zone** (on a soft dark vignette) carries ONE thing at a time, changing every 2 to 4 s: the hook title (small white
   line + huge butter "addictive"), a handwritten aside ("yes, even yours."), step chips "1 2 3 4" + "same example, all
   4 times", a retention line that dips ("step 2 · where they scroll away"), a **battery meter** with % (4% → 25% →
   50% → 75% → 96% → 100% "addicting") + a handwritten sublabel ("+ a consequence"), a white speech bubble ("so
   what?", "wait, that's me"), a search bar ("that one video" → "No Results"), a timer (18:36 → 20:00), a "Recently
   Deleted · 1 post, 0 motivation" notification, fill-in-the-blank template lines ("turns out the real problem was ___"),
   an Albums widget that fills, recap chips with a bookmark and send icon, "0 → 20,000 by Thanksgiving, come watch" +
   a profile card with a cursor clicking Follow.
4. **Blur pages** (about every 10 to 20 s, 2 to 4 s each): the face blurred and darkened, big stacked text: Poppins white
   lines + serif-italic butter keyword ("it's not the *topic* / it's how you *introduce it*", "that's what makes them
   **stop scrolling.**", "~~too many photos~~ no *system*", "same *topic.*", "the kind of content they *save.*" with a
   bookmark that fills, "make your audience *care*").
5. **Step cards**: full-screen butter yellow (or cream) with "step N" + progress dots, huge black Poppins with a serif
   italic word ("start with a *moment,* not a ~~topic~~."; "give the problem → a *consequence.*" with a hand arrow;
   "give them something they can actually *do.* ☐"). Each step's card is built word by word.
6. **Proof inserts**: a phone screen recording of the camera roll with a giant count (900 → 18,000 photos).
7. **Black-and-white flash** on the word "boring" (one second).
8. **Recap**: a yellow legal pad that writes out "ask yourself 4 questions" by hand, line by line, then crosses out
   "more interesting topic".

**Story** (why it holds for 2:23): see `/reel-edit` → "Storytelling". Hook with a promise + the objection answered,
thesis on a blur page by 11 s, the path mapped (4 steps, same example), one running example, a score that climbs, the
viewer's voice on screen, strike-throughs for the reframe, a saveable checklist, a send ask that names who, a series
reason to follow. Her caption gives the 4 lines as a fill-in template (moment / cost / turn / action).

**What we don't copy:** the 143 s length for news (our window is 25 to 45 s; a 4-step framework reel can run 60 to 90 s),
her bedroom set (ours stays dim room + practical light), her exact copy.

## Notes: striver-paper-style (@striver.tech, DfuMZKhBky9, 80 s), studied 2026-11-15: the paper look to keep
1. Warm off-white grid paper, no colour blocks. Face cam only as a small rounded photo card in the first 3 s (then it
   blurs out into the first page).
2. Top headline = the spoken sentence: one small sans line ("On November 12, Jensen Huang stepped down as NVIDIA's"),
   then a big **light serif italic** word/phrase under it ("*come*", "*joined the conversation*", "*1,600*", "*more
   transparency*"), sometimes coloured (blue, red, green). The next big word ghosts in grey before it's spoken.
3. ONE proof at a time, centred: **real** tweet embeds (NVIDIA Newsroom, Bryan Catanzaro, Elon Musk) with the key line
   highlighted yellow; article paragraphs as white quote cards with yellow highlights and underlined names; headline
   cards in bold sans; a photo with a name label pill ("Elon Musk", "Bryan Catanzaro · NVIDIA vice president"); a
   green date pill ("12 Nov 2026"); a mock "Suggestion box · internal post" card whose comment count climbs (1,404 →
   1,600+) with an arrow to the NVIDIA logo; envelope icons for "emails"; little people illustrations for "employees".
4. A new element every 2 to 3 s; elements fade/rise in; no full-screen colour pages.

## Notes: motionthekx-smooth (@motionthekx, DctTp5ay2Pa, 11 s), studied 2026-11-15 (4 frames/s): the motion to copy
1. Words arrive **out of a motion blur** (the incoming word is smeared and tinted, then snaps sharp); lines leave by
   defocusing ("A new beginning" blurs away).
2. A **word roller** ("Motion / Design / Ideas"): the list scrolls up, the active word sharp, the neighbours small and blurred.
3. A sentence builds word by word, keywords in red; then the text **collapses into an icon** (the Instagram logo), the
   icon **expands into a card** (profile card), and the **camera pushes in** on the card's Follow button (eased, ~0.7 s),
   an avatar rolls in and docks into the button ("Followed").
4. Everything eases (no linear moves, no hard pops); things overlap (the next element starts while the last one settles).
